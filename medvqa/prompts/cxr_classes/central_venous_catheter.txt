According to the report, the current CXR image(s) show a central venous catheter, meaning a catheter identified as a central venous access device whose course or tip is visible on the current radiographs. Explicit central venous catheter, CVC, central line, internal jugular or subclavian central catheter, dialysis catheter with central venous access, triple-lumen central catheter, implanted venous port, or port-a-cath supports this finding. A PICC also supports this class. A coiled, incompletely advanced, or otherwise malpositioned central venous catheter still supports device presence when currently visible. This includes a catheter intended for central venous access but inadvertently positioned in an artery. A true arterial monitoring catheter does not support this class. Tip position and malposition are assessed separately and do not negate presence. Do not infer a central venous catheter from a peripheral IV, arterial line, pulmonary artery or Swan-Ganz catheter, pacemaker or ICD leads, enteric or airway tube, chest tube, or line history or indication alone. Findings described only in clinical history or only on a prior examination do not establish this finding on the current CXR.
