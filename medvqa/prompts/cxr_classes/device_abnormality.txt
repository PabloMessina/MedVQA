According to the report, the current CXR image(s) show a radiographically visible abnormality of a medical device, meaning a currently visible therapeutic device has an abnormal course, location, or tip position, or a visible structural abnormality. Explicit malposition, misplacement, aberrant course, migration, retraction, unintended advancement, kinking, coiling, fracture, discontinuity, disconnection, or other wording characterizing the device or its position as abnormal supports this finding when applied to a support line, tube, catheter, drain, pacemaker or ICD lead, or other therapeutic device. Examples include an endotracheal tube described as too high, too low, mainstem, or esophageal; an enteric tube coiled in the airway or otherwise misplaced; a vascular catheter with an aberrant course or tip outside its intended location; a malpositioned chest tube; and a kinked, fractured, disconnected, or migrated device component. Do not infer this finding from device presence alone; from a numerical tip location without interpretation as abnormal; from clinical malfunction, occlusion, infection, or failure without a visible abnormality; from a complication occurring near an otherwise normally positioned and intact device, such as pneumothorax; from a statement that positioning is satisfactory or appropriate; or from device history alone. Complete removal or absence of a device does not constitute a device abnormality. Findings described only in clinical history or only on a prior examination do not establish this finding on the current CXR. Apply the general instructions for hedging, negation, temporality, and current-versus-prior findings.
