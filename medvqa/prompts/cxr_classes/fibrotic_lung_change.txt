According to the report, the current CXR image(s) show fibrotic lung change, meaning chronic fibrotic parenchymal remodeling beyond isolated focal scarring. Presence is supported by explicit pulmonary fibrosis or fibrotic/fibrosing parenchymal change; honeycombing; traction bronchiectasis or bronchiolectasis; architectural distortion, coarse reticulation, or volume loss attributed to fibrosis; a current UIP or fibrotic NSIP pattern; or current radiographic fibrotic changes attributed to IPF. Diffuse or extensive fibrotic scarring framed as fibrotic lung disease also supports this finding. Do not infer fibrotic lung change from isolated apical, linear, focal, or limited pleuroparenchymal scarring without broader fibrotic features. Do not infer it from a disease name or etiology alone; sarcoidosis, cystic fibrosis, asbestos exposure, tuberculosis, connective tissue disease, or prior radiation requires a current fibrotic finding. Generic reticular opacities, interstitial prominence or thickening, interstitial markings, linear opacities, or chronic lung disease do not support this finding unless attributed to fibrosis, and edema- or heart-failure-related interstitial findings do not qualify. Isolated pleural thickening, pleural plaques, fibrothorax, or non-traction bronchiectasis does not support fibrotic lung change without parenchymal fibrotic change. Findings described only in clinical history or only on a prior examination do not establish this finding on the current CXR. Apply the general instructions for hedging, negation, temporality, and current-versus-prior findings.
