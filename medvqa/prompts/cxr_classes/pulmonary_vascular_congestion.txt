According to the report, the current CXR image(s) show evidence of pulmonary vascular congestion when it explicitly describes pulmonary vascular, pulmonary venous, central vascular, hilar, or perihilar congestion or engorgement; congestive distention of pulmonary vessels; pulmonary venous congestion or hypertension; elevated pulmonary venous pressure; cephalization; upper-lobe venous diversion; or pulmonary vascular redistribution as a current radiographic finding. Pulmonary vascular plethora, indistinctness, haziness, blurring, or ill definition supports this finding only when attributed to vascular congestion, engorgement, or elevated pulmonary venous pressure, and pulmonary edema is not required when congestion is explicit. Do not infer pulmonary vascular congestion from hilar enlargement, prominent or enlarged central pulmonary arteries, pulmonary arterial hypertension, increased pulmonary blood flow, vascular plethora, cardiomegaly, pleural effusion, Kerley or septal lines, perihilar or diffuse airspace opacity, or pulmonary edema alone without explicit congestion or an equivalent such as cephalization or vascular redistribution. Findings described only in clinical history or only on a prior examination do not establish this finding on the current CXR. Apply the general instructions for hedging, negation, temporality, and current-versus-prior findings.
