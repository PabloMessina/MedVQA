According to the report, the current CXR image(s) show one or more focal osseous lesions, meaning discrete lytic, sclerotic, expansile, or otherwise abnormal lesions in a bone within the field of view. Explicit bone or osseous lesion, focal lytic or sclerotic lesion, expansile bone lesion, osseous metastasis, metastatic bone lesion, or multifocal osseous metastatic lesions supports this finding when attributed to the current radiographs. Benign, malignant, and indeterminate lesions all qualify. Do not infer a focal osseous lesion from an uncomplicated fracture, healing callus, or residual fracture deformity alone unless a separate underlying osseous lesion is stated. Do not infer it from osteopenia, osteoporosis, diffuse osseous sclerosis or demineralization, degenerative osteophytes, postsurgical change, or nonspecific bony irregularity alone. A soft-tissue, pulmonary, pleural, or mediastinal mass does not establish an osseous lesion without stated bone involvement. Findings described only on CT or MRI, only in clinical history, or only on a prior examination do not establish this finding on the current CXR. Apply the general instructions for hedging, negation, temporality, and current-versus-prior findings.
