According to the report, the current CXR image(s) show an elevated or eventrated hemidiaphragm, meaning a right or left hemidiaphragm is abnormally high or has a focal elevated contour compatible with eventration. Explicit elevation, high position, high-riding appearance, or eventration of a hemidiaphragm supports this finding when attributed to the current radiographs. Eventration may be focal or involve most of a hemidiaphragm. Do not infer this finding from phrenic nerve palsy, diaphragmatic weakness, or diaphragmatic paralysis alone without stated elevation or eventration. Do not count generalized bilateral diaphragmatic elevation attributed solely to low lung volumes, poor inspiration, or expiratory technique. Do not infer it from subpulmonic effusion, basilar opacity, atelectasis, abdominal distention, or another potential cause or mimic alone without explicit hemidiaphragmatic elevation. Apparent elevation attributed solely to a mimic does not establish this finding. Diaphragmatic flattening without elevation or eventration is also insufficient. Findings described only on CT, only in clinical history, or only on a prior examination do not establish this finding on the current CXR. Apply the general instructions for hedging, negation, temporality, and current-versus-prior findings.
