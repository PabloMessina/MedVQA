According to the report, the current CXR image(s) show abnormal fluid within the pleural space. Presence is supported by an explicit pleural effusion, pleural fluid, or hydrothorax of any size, laterality, or configuration, as well as hemothorax, empyema or pyothorax, chylothorax, and the fluid component of a current hydropneumothorax. Fissural or interlobar effusion and a pleural pseudotumor, phantom tumor, or vanishing tumor support presence when explicitly attributed to loculated fissural pleural fluid. A meniscus sign or costophrenic-angle blunting or obscuration supports presence only when attributed to pleural fluid. Do not infer an effusion from pleural thickening, plaques, scarring, fibrosis, an elevated hemidiaphragm, consolidation, atelectasis, pulmonary edema, pericardial effusion, or ascites without evidence of pleural fluid. Findings described only in clinical history or only on a prior examination do not establish this finding on the current CXR. Apply the general instructions for hedging, negation, temporality, and current-versus-prior findings.
