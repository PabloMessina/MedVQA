According to the report, the current CXR image(s) shows evidence of increased radiographic density within the lung parenchyma relative to normally aerated lung. Supporting findings include explicit pulmonary opacity, opacification, density, or shadowing of any distribution; consolidation; airspace or alveolar disease; infiltrate; atelectasis or collapse; parenchymal or interstitial pulmonary edema; ground-glass opacity; pulmonary nodule, mass, or granuloma; miliary, reticular, or reticulonodular opacity; pulmonary fibrosis; and any other finding explicitly representing increased pulmonary density. Explicit current radiographic pneumonia also supports Lung Opacity because parenchymal infection unavoidably entails an opacity, but a history or purely clinical mention of pneumonia does not. A cavity, abscess, cyst, or bulla supports opacity only when an opaque wall, contents, surrounding opacity, or underlying nodular or mass-like lesion is described. Pulmonary vascular congestion without parenchymal or interstitial opacity, pleural effusion, pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum, subcutaneous emphysema, support devices, extrapulmonary densities, artifacts, hyperlucency, emphysema, and thin-walled cysts or bullae without an opaque component do not support this finding. Base the assessment on current imaging; history-, indication-, differential-, or prior-only mentions do not support current presence, while a stable or unchanged opacity explicitly present on the current exam remains supportive.
