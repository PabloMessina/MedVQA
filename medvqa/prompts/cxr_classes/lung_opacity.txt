According to the report, the current CXR image(s) show evidence of increased radiographic density within the lung parenchyma relative to normally aerated lung. This broad class includes nonspecific pulmonary opacity and airspace, alveolar, interstitial, atelectatic, edematous, fibrotic, scar-like, nodular, or mass-like pulmonary patterns. Directly supporting findings include pulmonary opacity, opacification, density, or shadowing; airspace or alveolar opacity, disease, process, or opacification; infiltrate or infiltration; consolidation or consolidative change; ground-glass opacity; increased interstitial opacity, markings, or prominence; reticular, reticulonodular, or miliary opacity; Kerley or septal lines; atelectasis or pulmonary collapse; parenchymal or interstitial pulmonary edema; pulmonary nodule, mass, or granuloma; pulmonary fibrosis or scarring; and any other finding explicitly representing increased pulmonary parenchymal density. A radiographically asserted pneumonia entails Lung Opacity, whereas a purely clinical, historical, indication-only, or differential-only mention of pneumonia does not. A cavity, abscess, cyst, or bulla supports Lung Opacity only when an opaque wall or contents, surrounding pulmonary opacity, or an underlying nodular or mass-like lesion is described. Pulmonary vascular congestion without parenchymal or interstitial opacity, pleural effusion, pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum, subcutaneous emphysema, support devices, extrapulmonary densities, artifacts, hyperlucency, emphysema, and thin-walled cysts or bullae without an opaque component do not entail Lung Opacity.
