According to the report, the current CXR image(s) show costophrenic angle blunting, meaning loss of the normally sharp costophrenic angle, sulcus, or recess on an appropriate projection. Explicit blunting, obliteration, or obscuration of a costophrenic angle, CP angle, costophrenic sulcus, CP sulcus, costophrenic recess, or anatomically clear CPA supports this finding when attributed to the current radiographs. This includes an explicitly blunted lateral or posterior costophrenic angle or sulcus. Do not infer costophrenic angle blunting from pleural effusion, pleural thickening, pleural scarring, pleural plaques, an elevated hemidiaphragm, basilar opacity, atelectasis, or a meniscus sign alone without wording that the costophrenic angle, sulcus, or recess is blunted, obliterated, or obscured. Nonspecific obscuration of the lung base does not establish this finding. An angle that is outside the field of view, technically poorly visualized, or stated to be unassessable also does not establish blunting. Do not treat cardiophrenic-angle abnormalities as costophrenic-angle blunting. Findings described only on CT, in clinical history, or on a prior examination do not establish this finding on the current CXR. Apply the general instructions for hedging, negation, temporality, and current-versus-prior findings.
